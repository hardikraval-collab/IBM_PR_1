# Health Conditions & Dietary Guidelines

## Type 2 Diabetes Management

### Dietary Principles
- Prioritize low glycaemic index (GI) foods (GI < 55)
- Reduce refined carbohydrates significantly
- Distribute carbohydrates evenly across meals
- Increase dietary fibre intake (target 35-40g/day)
- Include protein with every meal to slow glucose absorption
- Avoid sugary drinks, fruit juices, and sweetened foods

### Recommended Foods for Diabetics
- Whole grains: oats, brown rice, barley, quinoa
- Non-starchy vegetables: broccoli, spinach, cauliflower, mushrooms
- Legumes: lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans (excellent for blood sugar control)
- Lean proteins: fish, chicken, tofu, Greek yoghurt
- Healthy fats: avocado, nuts, olive oil

### Foods to Strictly Limit
- White rice, white bread, potatoes, corn
- Sugary drinks, fruit juices, desserts
- Processed snacks, crackers, cookies
- Alcohol

### Meal Timing for Diabetics
- 3 meals + 2-3 small snacks to prevent blood sugar spikes
- Never skip meals
- Consistent meal times daily

## Heart Health & Cardiovascular Disease

### Dietary Principles
- Reduce saturated fat to < 7% of calories
- Eliminate trans fats completely
- Increase omega-3 fatty acids
- Increase dietary fibre, especially soluble fibre
- Reduce sodium to < 1500mg/day
- Follow Mediterranean or DASH-style eating patterns

### Heart-Protective Foods
- Fatty fish: salmon, sardines, mackerel (2 servings/week)
- Nuts: almonds, walnuts, pistachios
- Olive oil (replace butter and processed oils)
- Avocado
- Berries (antioxidant-rich)
- Dark leafy greens: spinach, kale, arugula
- Whole grains: oats, barley (soluble fibre reduces LDL)
- Legumes: lentils, chickpeas, beans

### Foods to Avoid for Heart Health
- Processed meats (sausages, hot dogs, deli meats)
- Fried foods and fast food
- Full-fat dairy in excess
- Coconut oil and palm oil in large quantities
- Refined carbohydrates and sugars

## Gut Health & Digestive Wellness

### Microbiome-Supporting Foods
**Probiotic-Rich Foods (live cultures):**
- Greek yoghurt, kefir
- Kimchi, sauerkraut, tempeh, miso
- Kombucha (low sugar)
- Buttermilk (chaas)

**Prebiotic Foods (feeds good bacteria):**
- Garlic, onions, leeks
- Oats (beta-glucan)
- Bananas (slightly unripe)
- Asparagus, artichokes
- Apples (pectin)

### High-Fibre Foods for Gut Health
- Legumes: 25-35g fibre/100g dry weight
- Chia seeds: 34g fibre/100g
- Avocado: 6.7g fibre/100g
- Broccoli: 2.6g fibre/100g
- Leafy greens: 2-4g fibre/100g

### Foods That Harm Gut Health
- Artificial sweeteners (aspartame, sucralose)
- Ultra-processed foods
- Excessive alcohol
- Excessive red meat
- Antibiotic overuse (disrupts microbiome)

## Weight Management

### Scientific Principles
- 1 kg of fat ≈ 7700 kcal deficit needed
- Safe rate of loss: 0.5–1 kg per week
- Caloric deficit of 300–500 kcal/day recommended
- Protein priority for muscle preservation during deficit

### DIP Diet Weight Loss Protocol
1. Calculate TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)
2. Create 300-500 kcal daily deficit
3. Protein target: 1.2-1.6g/kg body weight
4. Fill remaining calories with complex carbs and healthy fats
5. 16:8 intermittent fasting window
6. 150+ minutes moderate exercise per week

### Plateau-Breaking Strategies
- Calorie cycling (higher/lower days alternating)
- Increase NEAT (non-exercise activity thermogenesis)
- Re-evaluate portion sizes
- Ensure adequate sleep (7-9 hours critical for weight loss)
- Manage stress (cortisol promotes fat storage)

## Food Allergy Management

### Common Food Allergies (Big 8)
1. **Milk/Dairy:** Substitute with oat milk, almond milk, soy milk, coconut milk
2. **Eggs:** Substitute with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water), chia eggs, banana
3. **Peanuts:** Substitute with almond butter, sunflower seed butter, tahini
4. **Tree Nuts:** Substitute with seeds (pumpkin, sunflower, hemp)
5. **Wheat/Gluten:** Substitute with rice, quinoa, oats (certified GF), millet, buckwheat
6. **Soy:** Substitute with other plant proteins (pea protein, lentils, hemp seeds)
7. **Fish:** Substitute with flaxseeds/chia for omega-3; legumes for protein
8. **Shellfish:** Substitute with other lean proteins

### DIP Diet Allergy-Safe Swaps
- **Dairy-free protein:** Legumes + rice (complete amino acid profile), quinoa, hemp seeds
- **Gluten-free grains:** Quinoa, brown rice, millet, buckwheat, certified GF oats
- **Nut-free fats:** Avocado, olive oil, seeds, coconut

## Vegetarian & Vegan Nutrition

### Complete Protein Combinations (Plant-Based)
- Rice + Lentils (dal chawal) — complete amino acid profile
- Quinoa alone — complete protein
- Tofu + whole grains
- Hummus + whole wheat pita
- Edamame — complete protein

### Critical Nutrients for Vegans
| Nutrient | Best Plant Sources | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|-------|
| Vitamin B12 | Fortified foods | Supplement recommended |
| Vitamin D | Fortified foods, sunlight | Supplement often needed |
| Iron | Lentils, spinach, pumpkin seeds | Pair with Vitamin C |
| Calcium | Fortified plant milk, tofu, kale | Aim 1000mg/day |
| Omega-3 | Flaxseeds, chia, walnuts | Consider algae-based DHA |
| Zinc | Pumpkin seeds, chickpeas, cashews | Soak legumes to increase absorption |
| Iodine | Seaweed, iodized salt | Small amounts from seaweed |

## Intermittent Fasting Protocols

### 16:8 Protocol (DIP Diet Standard)
- Fast for 16 hours (includes sleep)
- Eat within an 8-hour window
- Example: Eat from 10am to 6pm
- Best for: general health, weight management, metabolic health

### 5:2 Protocol (Advanced)
- Normal eating 5 days per week
- Restrict to 500-600 kcal on 2 non-consecutive days
- Best for: accelerated weight loss, metabolic reset

### Breaking the Fast
- Break fast with something easy to digest: fruit, yoghurt, light soup
- Avoid heavy, rich meals immediately after fasting
- Protein-rich first meal helps preserve muscle

### Who Should NOT do Intermittent Fasting
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Children and teenagers
- People with history of eating disorders
- Individuals with Type 1 Diabetes (without medical supervision)
- Those who are underweight

**Always consult a healthcare provider before beginning any fasting protocol.**
