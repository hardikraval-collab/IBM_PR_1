# Regional Indian Food Guide for DIP Diet

## Introduction
DIP Diet is culturally inclusive and supports diverse regional cuisines. This guide maps traditional Indian foods to DIP Diet principles, making healthy eating accessible across all regions of India.

## North Indian Cuisine Adaptations

### DIP-Compatible North Indian Foods
- **Dal Makhani (lightened):** Reduce cream/butter by 80%. Use low-fat milk instead. High protein, fibre-rich.
- **Rajma Chawal:** Excellent plant protein + complex carbs combo. Use brown rice.
- **Chole:** Chickpeas are a perfect DIP Diet food — high protein, low GI.
- **Palak Paneer:** Use less cream. Spinach + paneer = iron + calcium powerhouse.
- **Moong Dal Chilla:** Thin green moong crepes — protein-rich breakfast.
- **Kadhi with Brown Rice:** Yoghurt-based kadhi is probiotic. Pair with brown rice.

### Make It DIP-Compliant
- Replace ghee with small amounts of cold-pressed mustard oil or olive oil
- Use whole wheat (atta) for rotis, parathas
- Avoid refined maida in all preparations
- Steam or bake instead of deep-frying
- Reduce oil by 50-70% in most recipes

## South Indian Cuisine Adaptations

### DIP-Compatible South Indian Foods
- **Idli:** Steamed rice cakes — easy to digest, low fat, moderate protein.
- **Dosa (plain):** Can use whole grain fermented batter. Low calorie.
- **Sambar:** Vegetable + lentil stew — excellent nutrition profile.
- **Rasam:** Digestive, anti-inflammatory, low calorie.
- **Vegetable Kootu:** Lentils + vegetables — balanced meal.
- **Pesarattu:** Green moong dosa — high protein, high fibre.

### Make It DIP-Compliant
- Use less oil for dosa — 1/2 tsp maximum
- Include sambar with every meal for protein and vegetable density
- Coconut chutney in small quantities (healthy fats)
- Fermented foods (idli/dosa batter, yoghurt) for gut health

## East Indian Cuisine Adaptations

### DIP-Compatible East Indian Foods
- **Dalma:** Lentils cooked with vegetables — Odisha specialty, DIP Diet ideal.
- **Chingri Macher Jhol (Prawn Curry):** Excellent lean protein for non-veg.
- **Shukto:** Bitter gourd vegetable medley — excellent for blood sugar control.
- **Sandesh/Mishti Doi:** Occasional treat — portion control essential.
- **Mustard Fish Curry:** Omega-3 rich, mustard oil has heart health benefits.

## West Indian Cuisine Adaptations

### DIP-Compatible West Indian Foods
- **Dhokla:** Steamed fermented snack — protein-rich, low calorie.
- **Thepla:** Whole wheat flatbread with vegetables — balanced.
- **Undhiyu:** Mixed vegetable dish — seasonal, nutrient-dense.
- **Kadhi Pakodi:** Lightened version without deep-frying pakodas.
- **Poha:** Light, easy to digest breakfast — use brown/red poha.

## Global Cuisine DIP Diet Adaptations

### Mediterranean
- Greek salad with olive oil
- Grilled fish with roasted vegetables
- Hummus with whole wheat pita
- Tabbouleh (parsley, bulgur, tomato)

### Asian
- Brown rice stir-fry with tofu and vegetables
- Miso soup (probiotic)
- Edamame as snack
- Soba noodles (buckwheat) with vegetables

### Western
- Overnight oats with berries
- Avocado toast on whole grain
- Grain bowls (quinoa/brown rice + protein + vegetables)
- Bean and vegetable chilli

## Seasonal Eating Guide

### Summer Foods
- Cucumbers, watermelon, musk melon, coconut water
- Light preparations: salads, raitas, cold soups
- Avoid heavy, oily, and spicy foods
- Increase hydration significantly

### Monsoon Foods
- Light, cooked vegetables (avoid raw leafy greens if possible)
- Warm soups and dals
- Fermented foods for gut immunity
- Avoid street food during this season

### Winter Foods
- Root vegetables: carrots, beets, turnips, sweet potato
- Warming spices: ginger, turmeric, black pepper, cinnamon
- Sesame seeds, jaggery (limited), dry fruits
- Warm cereals: khichdi, porridge, halwa (healthified versions)

## Cooking Methods Ranked by DIP Diet Preference
1. **Steaming** — Best. Preserves nutrients, no added fat.
2. **Poaching/Simmering** — Excellent for proteins and grains.
3. **Baking/Roasting** — Good. Minimal oil needed.
4. **Stir-frying** — Acceptable with minimal oil (1 tsp max).
5. **Sautéing** — Use non-stick pan, < 1 tsp oil.
6. **Pressure cooking** — Efficient for legumes, reduces cooking time and anti-nutrients.
7. **Deep frying** — Avoid or strictly limit to special occasions.

## Portion Size Reference Guide
| Food Type | One Serving |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cooked rice/grains | 1/2 cup (about 90g) |
| Dal/Legumes (cooked) | 1/2 cup (about 100g) |
| Vegetables | 1 cup raw or 1/2 cup cooked |
| Fruit | 1 medium fruit or 1/2 cup berries |
| Protein (paneer/tofu/meat) | 80-100g |
| Nuts | 30g (small handful) |
| Oil | 1 teaspoon per cooking instance |
| Whole grain roti | 1 medium (25-30g) |
